Experiments

Three small studies, each asking one question about motion. They are the practice behind the larger work above.

  1. ACyberPunkCan scroll behave like a timeline?
  2. BParallaxHow little does depth need?
  3. CGSAP MotionWhat makes motion feel orchestrated?

Study A · Scroll → canvas

CyberPunk

Can the page behave like a moving sequence instead of a document?

What it investigates

A 300-frame image sequence is drawn to a canvas and mapped to scroll progress. GSAP ScrollTrigger drives the pinned sections and Locomotive Scroll handles smooth scrolling, so the two have to stay in sync.

What it taught me

The hard part is not the effect but keeping a long frame sequence smooth while it shares a timeline with the rest of the page.

Toolkit

HTML, CSS, JavaScript, Canvas, GSAP, ScrollTrigger, Locomotive Scroll.

Study B · Pointer → depth

Parallax

How little does a flat screen need to feel like a place?

What it investigates

Separate mountain, fog and light layers each move at their own speed and perspective depth, with subtle rotation derived from the pointer. GSAP choreographs the opening.

What it taught me

Depth cues and a clear motion hierarchy do most of the work. Simple transforms, no 3D engine, and the scene already feels physical.

Toolkit

HTML, CSS, JavaScript, GSAP, perspective and translateZ. Photography in the scene is stock material.

Study C · Timeline → rhythm

GSAP Motion

What makes a set of animations feel orchestrated instead of decorated?

What it investigates

GSAP timelines sequence the opening and ScrollTrigger drives section motion, with scrubbed animation tied to scroll and cards that enter from a direction.

What it taught me

Animation is a system of timing and hierarchy. Small changes in sequencing, overlap and easing change how the whole interface feels.

Toolkit

HTML, CSS, JavaScript, GSAP, ScrollTrigger. The brand and copy in the page are demo content.
