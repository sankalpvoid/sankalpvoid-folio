Does it count as spending?

The ledger follows accounting rules rather than the order mail arrives. Make a call on each, then see what it does. Amounts are illustrative.

  • A card purchase₹1,200 at a restaurant, on the credit card

    Counts, on the day it happens. Cash only moves when the bill is paid, so card spending is shown as real spending whose bill is still to come.

  • Paying the card bill₹12,000 from the bank account

    Doesn’t count. It settles purchases already counted. Counting it again would double the expense, so it is shown as a card bill paid.

  • Moving money between your own accounts₹20,000, savings to current

    Doesn’t count. Own-account transfers, investments and income are not consumption.

  • A reminder that a SIP is due in two days₹5,000 instalment

    Not yet. A scheduled SIP is a commitment, and it counts only once money has moved. The reminder, the bank debit and the allocation stay linked but each keeps its own amount, so 5,000 and 4,999.75 are shown as they are, not smoothed.

Three decisions.

Say how old a number is.

The balance is the last one observed, labelled with its date and “Not a live balance”. A card’s amount due is not card spending, and a fund contribution is not portfolio value. Partial months are drawn hollow, and months before the ledger began are left out rather than shown as zero. Signed out, every stage carries an “Example data” tag.

One scale for every category.

The graph uses a single shared scale, so a taller curve always means a larger amount. Categories are never rescaled to fill the space. With fewer than five categories, only the real ones are drawn.

Fixed parsers, not an LLM.

The alert formats are known and fixed, so each has a deterministic parser. Nothing is sent to an AI provider and results are reproducible. A message that doesn’t match is marked unreadable and put in front of you for review.

Status

Where it stands. Concept, design and build. The introduction and a synthetic demo are public; the private workspace sits behind Google sign-in with per-user row-level security on Postgres. At the last release report (26 Oct) there were 170 tests, scrolling the intro forward and in reverse gave identical frames, and reduced motion, WebGL off and JavaScript off each get a static version of the same story.

Not verified. That report lists a real GPU, Safari, Firefox, physical phones, screen readers, and Google sign-in with Gmail sync end to end as not tested.
