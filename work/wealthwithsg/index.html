01 — The challenge

A chart is just noise until someone teaches you what to look for.

Market education usually starts with movement: a price, a pattern, a signal. The site’s line, written for the brand, points somewhere else: understand the market, not just the movement.

The communication problem was how to make a beginner feel that difference before reading a single lesson, and to do it without implying that a pattern is a prediction.

The learning problem is sequencing: noise first, then recognition, then structure, then the human judgement that structure never replaces.

04 — Decisions

What I chose, and why.

  1. 01

    SVG over WebGL

    The hero is hand-composed SVG driven by one scroll value. Layers fade and draw rather than render, so it stays light and sharp. It keeps separate compositions for desktop (64 small candles) and phones (26).

  2. 02

    Colour never carries meaning alone

    Bullish candles are green outlines and bearish are red, and each is also labelled in text. Candles are hollow, so the marks stay readable without relying on a colour distinction.

  3. 03

    Illustrative means labelled

    Every chart is stamped “Illustrative data · not market data”. There is no live feed and no implied signal, and a dedicated risk-disclaimer page exists.

  4. 04

    A narrative with a way out

    The journey is long, so “Explore learning” is available from the first frame and the navigation never leaves. The story is an invitation, not a gate.

  5. 05

    One real tool, not ten promises

    A risk-per-trade calculator is genuinely interactive: account size, risk percentage and stop distance become a position size. Pure arithmetic, nothing stored.

06 — Reflection

What I’d carry forward.

What I built

The visual system, the six-frame scroll narrative, the page templates and the interactive calculator, as a Next.js App Router project in TypeScript.

What remains

Real lessons, real articles and verified channels, supplied by the brand owner. Until then, the prototype says “sample” out loud rather than pretending.

What I learned

A long scroll story works when each frame changes the meaning of the same object. Honest labelling is part of the design, not a footnote to it.
