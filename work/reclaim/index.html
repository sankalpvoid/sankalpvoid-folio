Reclaim

A companion for the hard moments of quitting smoking, built around cravings, imperfect days and data that admits what it doesn’t know.

01 — Premise

Quitting is not one decision. It is a sequence of difficult moments.

The problem

Most smoking trackers count days or cigarettes. That is useful, but it only describes what already happened. It says nothing to someone in the middle of an urge at nine in the evening.

The situation I designed around

The exact moment someone is struggling, then the days after it: motivation shifting, triggers repeating, wins that are small and bad days that happen anyway.

The question

What can the app do in that moment, and what can it learn from that moment afterwards? That turns a counter into a feedback loop.

  2. 02One small action
  3. 03A record of it
  4. 04A pattern, over time

02 — Exploration

Not everyone is ready for the same commitment.

So Reclaim doesn’t ask for one. It starts with three paths, each with its own progress logic, and lets people change their pace later.

  • Quit

    Time smoke-free, an evidence-informed recovery timeline and savings, shown as context rather than pressure.

  • Reduce

    Targets, recorded days and periodic reviews. Cutting down is a legitimate route, not a failed attempt at quitting.

  • Track

    Daily counts, spending and a weekly pattern, for people who want to understand the habit before changing it.

03 — Decisions

Four choices that shaped the product.

  1. Decision 1

    Reduce is a route, not a failure.

    Reduction gets targets, recorded days and periodic reviews instead of being tucked behind a quit-only interface.

    The consequence: each journey carries its own progress logic.

  2. Decision 2

    A half-recorded day is not a clean day.

    Reduction reviews separate confirmed days from incomplete or untracked ones, so the product never rewards or punishes someone on the strength of data that isn’t there.

    In the product: reviews label days as confirmed, incomplete or untracked.

  3. Decision 3

    Treat cravings as data.

    A craving is something to get through, and over time a signal about timing, mood, trigger and which coping tool helped. The aim is insight from behaviour, not another motivational quote.

    Where it stands: a direction, not a finished capability. The quality of the insights is still being improved.

  4. Decision 4

    Local first, synced when possible.

    Core interactions work on the device and supported records sync when an account and backend are available. Real life is rarely a perfect connection.

    The consequence: progress lives on the device until someone logs in, so the home screen says so plainly.

What this is, and isn’t

A support tool, not treatment.

Reclaim supports behaviour change. It does not diagnose, treat or replace professional care, and the recovery timeline is general, evidence-informed guidance and estimates, not an individual medical claim.

This page makes no claims about effectiveness, adoption or health outcomes, because none are documented. Privacy is handled with user-owned data access and row-level security, and the community features carry moderation controls.

05 — Reflection

What exists, what doesn’t, what I took from it.

What I built

  • The product logic: three journeys, check-ins, craving toolkit, recovery milestones, savings goals
  • The interface, in dark and light themes
  • The front end, dependency-free JavaScript
  • Supabase auth, data and row-level security, plus analytics and regression tests (private repository)

Status, honestly

  • Working in the prototype: core journeys, behavioural logging, recovery and progress surfaces, craving support, stage-based community circles
  • Still improving: dashboard personalisation, insight quality, responsive consistency, accessibility, privacy UX
  • Not claimed: a finished product, outcomes or users

What I learned

  • Design for the imperfect day; most of the real product lives there.
  • Say what the data can’t tell you. Partial records are a design problem, not an edge case.
  • Smarter and calmer beats more screens. The roadmap is mostly subtraction.
