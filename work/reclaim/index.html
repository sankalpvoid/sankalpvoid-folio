  1. 01

    An urge arrives.

    The home screen leads with one question and one button, ahead of any counter.

  2. 02

    A small choice.

    Four small actions, no decision tree: box breathing, a five-minute wave, a water reset, a short walk. Or log the craving without an exercise.

  3. 03

    A small action.

    Box breathing: inhale, hold, exhale, hold, four seconds each. The recording below is the prototype itself.

  4. 04

    It’s recorded.

    Finishing logs the craving with the tool used. A one-tap “Did that help?” is optional and skippable. Everything is saved on the device and synced when you’re signed in.

  5. 05

    You come back.

    After a few rated cravings, a tool can be marked “Worked for you before”: a plain rule over the feedback you gave, not a validated or intelligent model. Sample data: four seeded cravings.

Three decisions

What shaped it.

01

Reduce is a route, not a failed quit.

Reduction has its own targets, recorded days and reviews, so people who aren’t ready to quit aren’t pushed through a quit-only interface.

02

A half-recorded day isn’t a clean day.

Reviews separate confirmed days from incomplete or untracked ones, so the product doesn’t reward or punish data that isn’t there.

03

Local first, synced when possible.

Core interactions work on the device; supported records sync when an account exists. The home screen says plainly that progress lives on the device until you log in.

Where it stands

A live prototype, still in development.

Working: three journeys, check-ins, the craving toolkit, recovery milestones, savings goals and community circles. Being improved: personalisation, insight quality, accessibility and privacy UX.

Reclaim is a support tool, not medical treatment, and the recovery timeline is general guidance. Screens use sample data; the source is private; nothing here claims effectiveness or adoption.
