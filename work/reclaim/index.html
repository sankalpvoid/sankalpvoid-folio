What can a quitting app do in the moment a craving arrives?

One evening.

Designed and built by me. Every screen below is the real prototype running locally with sample data.

  1. 01

    An urge arrives.

    The home screen leads with one question and one button, ahead of any counter.

  2. 02

    Support is one tap away.

    Four small actions, no decision tree: box breathing, a five-minute wave, a water reset, a short walk. Or log the craving without an exercise.

  3. 03

    A small action.

    Box breathing: inhale, hold, exhale, hold, four seconds each, with a paced orb. This is a recording of the prototype.

    Ready

    4 · 4 · 4 · 4 seconds

  4. 04

    It’s recorded.

    Finishing logs the craving with the tool used. A one-tap “Did that help?” is optional and skippable. Everything is saved on the device and synced when you’re signed in.

    Shown here: the on-device behaviour. Sign-in and sync run on Supabase in the private app and aren’t demonstrated on this page.

  5. 05

    You come back.

    After a few rated cravings, a tool can be marked Worked for you before. It is a plain rule over the feedback you gave: once three cravings are rated, the tool with the best average feedback (and at least two uses) moves to the top and gets the label. Not a validated or intelligent model.

    Sample data: four seeded cravings.

Progress, as context

Three decisions.

Reduce is a route, not a failed quit.

Reduction has its own targets, recorded days and periodic reviews, so people who aren’t ready to quit aren’t pushed through a quit-only interface. The consequence: each journey carries its own progress logic.

A half-recorded day isn’t a clean day.

Reduction reviews separate confirmed days from incomplete or untracked ones, so the product doesn’t reward or punish someone on data that isn’t there.

Local first, synced when possible.

Core interactions work on the device; supported records sync when an account is available. The home screen says plainly that progress lives on the device until you log in.

Status and notes.

Where it stands. A live prototype in active development. Working: three journeys, check-ins, the craving toolkit, recovery milestones, savings goals, stage-based community circles. Being improved: dashboard personalisation, insight quality, accessibility and privacy UX.

Good to know. Reclaim is a support tool, not medical treatment. The recovery timeline is general guidance and estimates. This page makes no claims about effectiveness or adoption. Screens use sample data. The source is private.
